About This Calculation

Flow in large ducts is measured by traversing a pitot tube across equal-area points (EPA Method 1), computing each point velocity from the velocity head and gas density, and averaging the velocities. Gas density comes from absolute pressure, temperature, dry molecular weight (Orsat) and moisture (EPA Methods 2 and 3).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why average velocities and not velocity heads?

Velocity goes as the square root of velocity head, so the mean of square roots is smaller than the root of the mean; averaging heads would overstate flow.

What pitot coefficient should I use?

Use the calibration certificate; typical values are 0.84 for an S-type and about 0.99 for a standard Prandtl pitot.

How many traverse points are needed?

EPA Method 1 specifies the number from the duct diameter and the distance to the nearest flow disturbance; typically 12 to 24 points for a circular duct. More points reduce the error from an uneven velocity profile.

Why is molecular weight needed?

Gas density sets the velocity for a given velocity head. Flue gas is heavier than air because of CO₂ and water vapour; the Orsat analysis and moisture content give the molecular weight and so the density.
